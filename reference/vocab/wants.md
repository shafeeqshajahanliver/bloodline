## Hardest cases

- **Love, lust and keeping people.** Three fears sit close together. "a lover to possess" is attachment to one partner (including jealous love, removing a rival, and ghosts who stay with a husband or lover: Nang Nak, Pee Mak, Shutter, Atlantics). "lust and indulgence" is appetite without attachment (desire, sex, "primal desire" in The Howling and Shivers, "pleasure" in Dorian Gray and From Beyond). "keeping people forever" is a non-romantic grip: parents who will not release a child ("possession of the son" in Psycho, Braindead, Santa Sangre, Lights Out), houses and spirits that trap guests, Annie's murder-suicide plan in Misery. "to rape women" (Sumpah Orang Minyak, the devil's demand) went to lust as sexual violence; it is not love.
- **Revenge versus punishing sin.** Personal or group grievances ("revenge on men", "revenge on teachers", "to be paid" in Atlantics, Stree's "to take men") are revenge. Values where the threat claims a moral or divine mandate ("purge of the depraved", "to kill a witch" for Margaret White in Carrie, "to destroy hidden demons" in Frailty, "a sign from god" and "to save a soul" in Saint Maud, "to liberate souls" in The Cremator) form "punishing sin". "nature's revenge" and "to punish humans" stayed under revenge as punishing humanity, because the films frame them as payback for harm done.
- **Spirits that want a person: claiming, sacrifice or feeding?** "claiming souls and victims" covers threats that come to collect someone marked or owed (Veronica, The Haunting, Carnival of Souls, In Fabric's dress, Final Destination's "to restore its plan", Pyewacket's wish fulfilled). "the sacrifice demanded" covers a victim given up by people for a ritual or harvest. "feeding on the living" covers eating, including Freddy's and Pennywise's feeding on fear and Smile's "trauma".
- **The grieving and the replacing.** "to replace a lost child" and "to replace a lost lover" went to "getting back the lost" (grief), while plain "to replace someone" (Dead of Night's dummy, The Housemaid) and doppelgängers went to "taking someone's place" (usurping).
- **Other single calls.** "a mate" (Bride of Frankenstein) went to loneliness, not lust. "a new skin" (Buffalo Bill) went to transformation. "to drink the killer's blood" (Manichitrathazhu) is revenge, not feeding. "new blood" and "outsiders for breeding" (Frontier(s)) are continuing a bloodline. "to end the bloodline" (Usher) and "her skin" (Impetigore) are about lifting a curse. "a gifted person" and "songs and stories" (Remmick in Sinners) were read as recruiting and longing for connection.
- **Unclear (3 values).** "nothing stated" (18 films), "innocent curiosity" (Frankenstein's creature reaching for sunlight) and "to relive the past" (The Pit and the Pendulum).
