# Ending: how the story resolves

26 fears, 162 kinds, 549 values. Grouped by outcome: whether the threat is destroyed, cast out, laid to rest, survives, spreads, is handed on, or left open are kept as separate fears. Counts are values and the sum of films using them.

## the evil lives on

The threat is still alive or free at the end, or seems beaten and is not; nothing is passed on, it simply endures.

34 values, about 162 film uses.

Kinds: threat survives (9); threat escapes (6); threat may return (5); evil triumphs (4); defeat proves false (3); last-minute scare (3); attempt to stop it fails (3); curse holds (1)

## Hardest cases

- **escape, threat survives**: placed under *the evil lives on*, not *getting out alive*, because the threat's fate is what the ending dimension separates; the same logic put *survivors leave, new ghost remains* under *the evil is handed on*.
- **threat escapes with victim** (Let the Right One In: Oskar leaves with Eli): could be the threat surviving or the hero joining it; placed under *victims become the evil* (victim sides with the threat), since the protagonist chooses the monster.
- **hunters defeated** (Byzantium): the vampire protagonists beat the men hunting them, so read as *the guilty are punished* (persecutors destroyed) rather than *the evil lives on*.
- **threat departs** (Lifeforce) and **threat returns to nature** (The Lure): the threat leaves on its own and is not destroyed or laid to rest, so *the evil lives on* (threat escapes); **threat appeased** (satisfied, then leaves) went to *the dead find peace*.
- **false resolution**, **false escape**, **threat seemingly dispelled**: the apparent victory is undone, so *the evil lives on* (defeat proves false), never *the evil is destroyed*.
- **creator executed** (The Curse of Frankenstein, nobody believes his story): punishment of the guilty outweighs the disbelief, so *the guilty are punished*.
- **house destroyed**: kept as *the place is destroyed* rather than *the evil is destroyed*, since the wording names the place, not the threat.
- **victim becomes ghost** placed under *the protagonist dies*, not *victims become the evil*: a ghost is not necessarily a threat.
- **survivors refuse the sacrifice** (The Cabin in the Woods): the outcome is the end of the world, but the value names the choice, so *a test of conscience*.
- **unclear** (4): creation comes to life (May), guardian appointed (Sumpah Orang Minyak), rejecting the parent (Opera), servant killed by master (Renfield in Dracula).
