Where nightmares live

Every culture keeps telling horror stories. This report asks which ones, where, and when: the twelve nightmares the graph model found, and the archetypes set from the top down, by region and by era.

The headline

The West fears the living. Asia fears the dead.

Two nightmares split the world almost cleanly. Killers and captives, the fear of people who trap and kill, belongs to the West. The restless dead, the wronged who return, belongs to Asia.

West: North America, Continental Europe, UK & Ireland, Australia & NZ (373 films). Asia: East, Southeast and South Asia (95 films). The archetypes, tagged by a different method, show the same split: the woman who comes back is in 20% of Asian films and 2% of Western ones.

Cults and curses is the rising nightmare

Almost absent before 1960, it is now the most common nightmare in new horror: the group that turns on you, the dark god, the cursed thing passed on.

1 of 41 films before 1960 → 26 of 147 since 2015

The monsters are leaving

Blood drinkers have nearly vanished, and creatures make up a shrinking share of new horror. The fear has moved inside: doubles and impostors, and the voice inside, are climbing.

Blood drinkers 17% → 2% · Doubles and impostors 5% → 12%

Southeast Asia is possessed

Possession turns up everywhere, but nowhere as often as in Southeast Asian horror, where a third of the films are about something getting in.

10 of 33 Southeast Asian films · 10% everywhere

Folk horror is British

The village that needs blood, and cults and curses more broadly, gather in British and Irish films: the community as the threat.

The village needs blood: 6 of 58 UK & Irish films · 3% everywhere

How to read this, and what to doubt

  • The films. 497 horror films chosen for global breadth. It is a curated list, not a random or ranked sample, and it leans towards North America (223 films). Shares are within each region or era, so the larger groups do not drown the smaller ones, but a region's share is only as good as the films chosen for it.
  • Nightmares are the twelve groups a graph model found from the fears the films share. Each film belongs to one. The model was not shown the regions, the eras or the archetypes.
  • Archetypes are story shapes Bloodline classified from the top down. Here each film counts once, under its main archetype.
  • Thin groups. South Asia (16 films), Latin America (18), Australia & NZ (15), the Middle East & North Africa (7) and Sub-Saharan Africa (4) are small. A single film moves their shares by several points.
  • First reading. The fears behind the nightmares and the archetype tags are a first machine reading, not yet checked by hand. Treat every finding here as a strong lead.
