# First trends across the corpus

Medians by era. Dialogue figures use only English subtitle files whose length matches the film's runtime (343 films). Jump-scare data is crowd-sourced and mostly covers US and UK films (189 films), so read it as Anglophone horror.

| Era | Words per minute | Share of runtime with dialogue | Longest silence | First jump scare | Scares per film | Runtime |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before 1960 | 105 | 50% | 3.1 min | (2 films) | – | 82 min |
| 1960–79 | 75 | 40% | 2.2 min | 27.8 min | 4 | 95 min |
| 1980–99 | 81 | 40% | 2.4 min | 19.1 min | 7 | 99 min |
| 2000–14 | 75 | 40% | 3.1 min | 18.0 min | 9 | 100 min |
| 2015–25 | 74 | 40% | 2.8 min | 18.0 min | 5 | 101 min |

## What stands out

1. **Early horror talked.** Films before 1960 run at around 105 words a minute with dialogue over half the film. From the 1960s horror gets quieter and stays there.
2. **The first scare moved forward by ten minutes,** from about 28 minutes in the 1960s and 70s to about 18 minutes from 1980 on, and it has held there for 40 years.
3. **Jump scares peaked and retreated.** Scares per film climb to a median of 9 in 2000–14, then fall to 5 since 2015. That fits the rise of slower "elevated" horror (Hereditary, The Witch, It Follows), and it's the most testable claim here.
4. **Horror films got longer:** a median of 82 minutes before 1960, about 100 now.

## Caveats

- Era sizes differ (39 good subtitle files before 1960, 91 in 2000–14).
- Subtitle word counts depend on the subtitler; medians smooth this but don't remove it.
- The full-film visual layer (colour, darkness, editing pace) exists only for Nosferatu. It needs film files to scale.
